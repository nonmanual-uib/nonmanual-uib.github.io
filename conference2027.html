Keynote speakers

Anna Puupponen

Anna Puupponen is an Academy Research Fellow at the Sign Language Centre, University of Jyväskylä, Finland. Her research focuses on the embodied, semiotic, and social aspects of sign language communication, with particular interests in depiction, reference, indexicality, social variation, and language processing. Her work has explored the multi-articulatory nature of signed discourse, variation across signers of different ages, and the acquisition and brain-based processing of Finnish Sign Language (FinSL), drawing on methods from corpus linguistics, linguistic ethnography, kinematic movement analysis, and cognitive neuroscience. She currently leads the project Indexicality in Language: Insights from Use, Learning and Processing of Finnish Sign Language (InSIGN, 2026-2030), which investigates how first-language signers and second-language learners of FinSL use visible bodily actions to indicate referents and manage turn-taking in discourse, and how they process spatial indexicality when perceiving and producing sign language.

Wim Pouw

Wim Pouw is a cognitive scientist at Tilburg University at the Department of Computational Cognitive Science studying different aspects of movement and communication. His overall research program is about understanding how communication systems are forged through multi-scale interactions that become coordinated (e.g., functionally aligned) in a way that allows for more novel, stable, adaptable, or more evolvable communication to emerge. Ultimately, he is empirically and theoretically investigating the pre-theoretical intuition that to understand how humans produce meaningful utterances we must ground it in more domain-general multi-scale processes that are shared with other animals. His expertise lies in quantitative approaches of body movement analysis (e.g., motion tracking methodology, time series analysis) with tracking of other communicative means such as vocalizations and speech. He combines a wide range of theoretical (radical embodied cognitive science, dynamical systems, evolutionary biology) and methodological (e.g., data science, movement science, bioacoustics, computer vision, phonetics) interests in his research so as to come to a computationally reproducible and theoretically grounded understanding of the topics he and his colleagues study. In addition to his research, Wim Pouw leads open science platform envisionbox.org and is involved in other open science efforts in scholarly publishing.
